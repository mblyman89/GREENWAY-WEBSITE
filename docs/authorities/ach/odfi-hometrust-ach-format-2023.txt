         Introduction
         This NACHA File Format Specifications guide provides file record layouts for formatting ACH entries
         your company originate through HomeTrust Bank’s Treasury Management Services using common
         standard entry class codes (SECs). It is very important that you (or your software vendor) utilize the
         specifications outlined in this guide. This will ensure a successful file transmission.

         The formats and specifications described in this guide comply with the rules of the National Automated
         Clearing House (NACHA). NACHA Rules bind all participants utilizing the automated clearing house
         (ACH) and compliance with the rules is mandatory. For a definition of terms or more information
         regarding ACH, please refer to your NACHA Rule Book. If you do not have a copy, you may access the
         rules online at: www.achrulesonline.org.

         Testing
         Please contact your HomeTrust Bank representative once you are ready to begin transmitting your ACH
         files. In addition, if you plan to change key fields in either of the Header Records please contact us. These
         types of changes may require additional testing.

         ACH Sequence of Records




                There are 94 characters in an ACH record
                ACH records are numbered: 1, 5, 6, 7 if applicable, 8, and then 9
                There are 10 records in a block
                Within each file, there can be multiple batches
                Within each batch, there can multiple detail entry and addenda records
                Addenda records provide additional information pertaining to the entry detail record they are
                 attached to

1.        '5' designates a Company/Batch Header Record. This record identifies the Originator and briefly describes the purpose for the batch of
          transactions. All data in this record applies to all detail records within this batch.

2.        Service class code is '200' if both debits and credits are contained in this batch, '220' if all credits and '225' if all debits.

3.        Contains the name of your company. This field will be shown by the receiving bank on its statement or advice to recipient.

          HomeTrust's Requirement:
          If you ever change this information, you must notify HomeTrust in advance as we rely on it to identify your company and your settlement
          account number.

4.        This field may contain descriptive data, but it may not be printed by the receiving bank on its statement or advice to recipient.

5.        Your company IRS employer ID number**, preceded by the number '1' should be in this field. Alternately, you may use a DUNS number
          preceded by the number '3', or a unique number preceded by any number, '0' - '9'. Used to identify an Originator.
          **HomeTrust's Preference.

          HomeTrust's Requirement:
          If you ever change this information, you must notify HomeTrust in advance as we rely on it to identify your company and your settlement
          account number.

6.        Use the appropriate code to designate the type of transaction (other codes are in the NACHA rule book):
          PPD - Prearranged Payments and Deposits (Corporate to Consumer)
          CCD - Cash Concentration or Disbursement (Corporate to Corporate)
          CTX - Corporate Trade Exchange (Corporate to Corporate)

7.        Company entry description should describe the type of transaction; i.e 'Payroll', 'Salary', 'Water Bill', etc. Use general terms since this
          description will apply to all detail records within the batch. The contents of this field will be shown by the receiving bank on its statement
          or advice to recipient.

8.        A date meaningful to your employee or customer, such as payroll period ending date.

9.        The date on which this transaction should post to the receiver's account.

10.       Reserved, leave blank. Federal Reserve will insert settlement date.

1.        '6' designates an entry detail record. Entry detail records contain information needed to post transaction.

2.        Choose the appropriate code to identify the type of DR and CR entry:
          '22' = credit to checking account
          '23' = prenotification of credit to checking
          '24' = "0" credit with remittance to checking
          '27' = debit to checking account
          '28' = prenotification of debit to checking
          '29' = "0" debit with remittance to checking
          '32' = credit to savings account
          '33' = prenotification of credit to savings
          '34' = "0" credit with remittance to savings
          '37' = debit to savings account
          '38' = prenotification of debit to savings
          '39' = "0" debit with remittance to savings

          NOTE: The most frequently used Transaction Codes are "22," "27," "32," and "37." If you plan to conduct a pre-note test, you MUST use
          one of the following Transaction Codes, based upon the nature of the transaction: "23," "28," "33," or "38." Use Transaction Code "27"
          for INBOUND activity (i.e. you are RECEIVING funds) and "22" for OUTBOUND activity (i.e. you are DISBURSING funds). As an
          example, if the Transaction Code "27" is used, this activity will affect the RECEIVER'S checking account, not your Settlement account.
          Accordingly, a transaction with the code "27" will result in a debit to the receiver's checking account and a credit to your settlement
          account.

3.        The receiving bank's R/T number, where TTTT = routing transit number and AAAA = ABA number. This indicates the bank to which this
          transaction will be routed.

4.    The check digit is the 9th (and last) digit of the bank routing transit number in 3 above. The check digit is used to assure that the

6.    The amount of the transaction in dollars and cents, with no punctuation. High order zeros will be used. If this entry is a prenote, then this

      # of addenda records
      Company Name
      Reserved

9.    Optional Data field.

11.   The trace number is an ascending consecutive number used to uniquely identify each transaction. The first eight digits of the trace number
      consist of the bank R/T number as described for Field 12 of the Company Batch Header Record '5'. The last seven digits of the trace
      number consist of a sequential number in ascending sequence with each subsequent transaction incremented by 1. Example:
      253170270000001, 253170270000002, 253170270000003

3.        The number of Entry Detail Records plus each Entry Detail Addenda Records in this batch

          HomeTrust's Preference:
          Your entry detail records should not contain a settlement entry. This is an automated entry at HomeTrust.

          HomeTrust's Preference:
