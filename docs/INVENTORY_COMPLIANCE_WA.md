# WA Retail Cannabis Inventory Management — Compliance Reference (Task L)

> **Purpose.** Everything a Washington I-502 cannabis **retailer** (Greenway, license
> 413541, Port Orchard) must do to manage inventory in a CCRS- and DOH-compliant way,
> compiled from primary sources only — WAC/RCW full texts (`/workspace/research/wac`,
> `/research/rcw`, `/research/doh`), the WSLCB CCRS Upload User Guide, the CCRS Data
> Model spec, and live scrapes of app.leg.wa.gov (Jan 2026). Companion to
> `docs/COMPLIANCE_BIBLE.md`. NEVER GUESS — every claim below cites its source.

## 1. Traceability — WAC 314-55-083(4)

- Licensees must track cannabis **seed to sale** and keep required information
  **"completely up-to-date in a system specified by the LCB"** (CCRS).
- For a retailer the traceable events/records include:
  - **(g) A complete inventory** of all cannabis products on the premises;
  - (d) transport events (receiving manifests);
  - (e) **any theft** of product;
  - (h) all products **physically tagged with the unique identifier** generated by the
    traceability system and tracked;
  - (i) all point-of-sale records;
  - (j) excise-tax records incl. medical-exemption records;
  - (k)–(o) samples of every kind (lab, vendor, quality, LCB compliance checks).
- Consequence for our system: **inventory on-hand, adjustments, sales, transfers, and
  samples must all reconcile** — the CCRS picture must match physical reality. Failure to
  use/maintain traceability is a **Category II violation** ($1,250 → license cancellation,
  WAC 314-55-520); traceability data issues can also land Category V.

## 2. Recordkeeping — WAC 314-55-087 (verified via live scrape, current text)

- Records kept **on the licensed premises for a five-year period** (companion rule
  314-55-089(1)(e) restates 5 years; the Bible's "current + previous five" phrasing comes
  from the earlier text — we retain **at least 6 years** to be safe) and made available to
  LCB on request. Retailer-relevant record classes:
  - (a) **purchase invoices** and supporting documents (items, from whom, date);
  - (k) **inventory records**;
  - (p) records of **any theft**;
  - (q) product **provided free to qualifying patients/providers**;
  - (r) **detailed sale records** (date, price, item, taxes);
  - (s) medical excise-exemption records (per 314-55-090).
- **(2) POS/ADP systems are explicitly regulated**: if records live in a POS, the system
  must (a) provide an **audit trail** so details underlying summary data can be produced,
  (b) allow **tracing any transaction back to source or forward to a final total**, and
  (c) have a full description of the system's applications, procedures, and controls.
  ⇒ our `inventory_adjustments` ledger + `recordAudit` trail + immutable CCRS archive ARE
  the audit trail; nothing inside the retention window may be hard-deleted.
- Violation = **Category IV** (WAC 314-55-523).

## 3. Reporting & the "undocumented shrink = taxed as a sale" rule — WAC 314-55-089

- (4)(a) Retailers must **monthly** maintain records and report purchases from processors,
  **sales by product type**, and **lost and/or destroyed product** in the manner
  prescribed by LCB (keeping CCRS completely up-to-date fulfills this for
  producers/processors per (2)/(3); retailers file LIQ-1295 + CCRS uploads).
- **(4)(c) — THE BIG ONE: "Product inventory reductions that are not adequately documented
  will be deemed to be sales and will be assessed the excise tax."**
  ⇒ Every reduction in on-hand MUST be documented with a reason (sale, adjustment with
  reason + note, destruction, return, sample). Unexplained shrink is a 37% excise
  liability plus penalties. This is the compliance backbone of cycle counts: variances
  found and posted as documented `Reconciliation` adjustments are "adequately documented";
  silent drift is not.

## 4. CCRS reporting mechanics (Upload User Guide, verified against local corpus)

- CSV upload only, **weekly** cadence (week Sun–Sat, due by following Sunday); files only
  needed when there is unreported activity.
- Retailer files: `Area`, `Strain`, `Product`, `Inventory`, `InventoryAdjustment`,
  `InventoryTransfer`, `Sale`. Dependency order: references (Strain/Area/Product) ≥10
  minutes before Inventory; Inventory before Adjustment/Transfer/Sale.
- **Inventory.csv** — represents physical inventory: `LicenseNumber, Strain, Area,
  Product, InitialQuantity, QuantityOnHand, TotalCost, IsMedical, ExternalIdentifier, …`.
  Additions from receiving report here (NOT as adjustments).
- **InventoryAdjustment.csv** — records events that increase/decrease on-hand:
  - `AdjustmentReason` valid values (guide p.30): **Destruction, Reconciliation, Lost,
    Seizure, Theft, Other** (+ **ReturnedLabSample** per the reason-mapping table and data
    model).
  - **`AdjustmentDetail` REQUIRED when reason = Other or Theft** (250 chars).
  - **Quantity: decimal, "No Negative entries allowed"** — direction is carried by the
    event; magnitude only. UOM consistent with the inventory type.
  - `AdjustmentDate` MM/DD/YYYY.
- Our internal → CCRS mapping (implemented in
  `src/lib/compliance/ccrs-inventory-adjustment-core.ts`, self-tested):
  `destruction→Destruction · count→Reconciliation · shrink/damage→Lost ·
  sample→ReturnedLabSample · employee_sample→Other (detail names the employee; LCB-
  confirmed) · recall→Destruction · theft→Theft · seizure→Seizure · receive→excluded
  (Inventory.csv) · else→Other`.
- Returns from customers: DELETE the sale row, then report an InventoryAdjustment —
  never a negative sale.
- **LabTestExternalIdentifier mandatory since Oct 1, 2025** — a lot without a linked lab
  identifier cannot be compliantly reported/sold (our lot-activation gate enforces this).

## 5. Retailer operating rules that shape inventory — WAC 314-55-079

- **(10) Maximum four months of average inventory on the licensed premises at any given
  time.** ⇒ the system should surface months-of-supply so over-stocking is visible.
- (7) Never sell below current **acquisition cost** (ties discounts to `unit_cost_minor_units`).
- (8) All cannabis product stored **behind a counter or barrier** — no direct customer access.
- **(12) Customer returns of open product allowed ONLY in original packaging with the lot,
  batch, or inventory ID number fully legible** ⇒ returns must attach to a lot.
- (11)/(13) Retailer may transport to own locations / return product to a processor per
  -085, and dispose of product per -097.

## 6. Destruction & waste — WAC 314-55-097 (live scrape, current text incl. 2025 revision)

- Retail cannabis waste (not dangerous/hazardous) must be **rendered unusable before
  leaving the premises**: ground and mixed ≥50% non-cannabis material by volume
  (compostable or non-compostable mix), then disposed at a permitted facility.
- **Records of the final destination and method for ALL cannabis waste rendered unusable
  must be maintained** (subsections (4)(c), (7)(c)) and kept per -087.
- CCRS: destruction is an InventoryAdjustment reason `Destruction`; the codebase applies a
  **72-hour quarantine hold** before completing destruction
  (`DESTRUCTION_QUARANTINE_HOURS` in `src/lib/inventory/disposition.ts`) so LCB has
  advance notice of the pending event — the hold satisfies the historical notice practice;
  the destruction event itself must be reported in the weekly file.
- "Sellable cannabis waste" (≤0.3% THC) provisions target producers/processors, not retail.

## 7. Transport / receiving — WAC 314-55-085

- Shipment notification + **receipt of shipment reported in the traceability system**
  ((1)/(2)) — receiving a manifest MUST update CCRS inventory (our InventoryTransfer +
  Inventory files).
- Printed transport manifest with the product at all times; **transport records kept
  minimum 3 years**; sealed packages; direct routes; 21+ transporter. Our
  `inbound_manifests` transport fields capture chain-of-custody.

## 8. Quality assurance — WAC 314-55-102 / lot activation

- Product **"must not be converted, transferred, or sold by the licensee until the
  required tests are reported"** ((2)(c)). Retail consequence: a lot without a passing lab
  result must not be sellable ⇒ our Slice-107 lot-activation gate (failed/missing lab ⇒
  quarantine) is a direct control for this rule.
- COAs archived and retrievable (records rule) — `lab_results.coa_storage_path`.

## 9. Medical / DOH — chapter 246-70 WAC (endorsed retailer)

- **Compliant product classes** (246-70-040/-050): General Use / High THC / High CBD, each
  with per-serving/per-unit caps and extra QA (pesticide + heavy-metal screening).
- **Labeling** (246-70-060): DOH logo (black/dark blue) per class; "not approved by the
  FDA…" statement; no medical-benefit claims.
- Inventory consequence for an endorsed retailer (WAC 314-55-080/RCW 69.50.375):
  - must **ALWAYS keep DOH-compliant product in stock or on order** — the system should
    flag when `is_medical` on-hand hits zero;
  - `is_medical` (DOH-compliant) flag on lots drives the excise/sales-tax exemption path
    and CCRS `IsMedical` on Inventory.csv — the flag must be set at intake (Cultivera/WCIA
    `Is Medical`) and preserved through counts/adjustments.
- Exempt-sale records retained ≥5 years (314-55-090).

## 10. Security backdrop — WAC 314-55-083(1)-(3)

- CCTV over all areas where cannabis is **moved, stored, or destroyed** and all POS areas
  (45-day retention). Cycle counts and destructions happen on camera; the system's
  timestamps (NIST-aligned server time) corroborate the footage.

## 11. Violation exposure map (WAC 314-55-520…-523)

| Failure | Category | First penalty |
|---|---|---|
| Traceability not used/maintained | II | $1,250 → cancellation |
| Inventory records missing (087) | IV | $500+ |
| Undocumented reductions | — | deemed sales ⇒ 37% excise + penalties (089(4)(c)) |
| Below-cost sale / free product | IV | $500–1,250 |
| Returns without legible lot ID | V | tiered |
| Sampling violations | IV | $1,250 |
- **Mitigator (314-55-509): documented business policies/practices that reduce risk** —
  printed SOPs, blind counts, variance review, and audit trails are formal penalty
  mitigators.

## 12. What "inventory managed like a pro" means here (best-practice layer)

Industry-standard techniques adopted by the Task L build (these are professional practice,
not legal requirements — the legal floor is sections 1–11):

1. **Perpetual inventory + cycle counting** (never wall-to-wall-only): count subsets on a
   cadence so every SKU is verified multiple times a year without closing the store.
2. **ABC classification** by inventory value at cost: A-lots ≈ top 80% of cumulative
   on-hand value, B ≈ next 15%, C ≈ last 5%. Count A most often (monthly), B quarterly,
   C semi-annually — highest-value stock gets the most audit attention.
3. **FEFO (First-Expired, First-Out)**: sell-first priority ranked by expiry date, then
   age. Cannabis is perishable and WA lots carry `expires_on`; expired product is
   unsellable waste (documented destruction) — every day of early rotation is margin.
4. **Aging & velocity awareness**: bucket lots by days-on-hand (0–30/31–60/61–90/90+);
   90+ day cannabis inventory is a markdown/return-to-vendor candidate before it becomes
   destruction paperwork.
5. **Variance discipline**: blind counts (counter never sees the system figure),
   manager-level variance review BEFORE posting, and flagged recounts when a line variance
   exceeds thresholds (any variance on A-lots; larger tolerance on C-lots). Count accuracy
   percentage tracked per session over time.
6. **Shrink telemetry**: report shrink (shrink/damage/theft/lost adjustments) as both
   units and value at cost per period; unexplained shrink is the exact thing WAC
   314-55-089(4)(c) taxes, so the target is 0 undocumented, and documented shrink trends
   drive process fixes.
7. **Months-of-supply guardrail**: on-hand ÷ average monthly consumption per category,
   surfaced against the **4-month regulatory ceiling** (WAC 314-55-079(10)).
8. **Exception-first UI**: the daily driver screen leads with what needs attention
   (expired, expiring, missing COA, negative/zero on-hand, unmapped, recalled, quarantine,
   overdue-for-count) rather than a flat table.
